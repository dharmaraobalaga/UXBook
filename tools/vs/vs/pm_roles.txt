Product Manager vs Product Designer vs UX Designer vs UI Designer
These four roles work closely together to build a successful digital product, but each role has a different responsibility.

Let's understand them using a practical example: building a food delivery app like Swiggy or Zomato.

1. Understand the four roles at a glance

Role                   Main responsibility                                Key question

Product Manager        Product direction and business success             What should we build, and why?
(PM)

Product Designer       End-to-end product experience and problem-         How should the product solve the
                       solving                                            problem?

UX Designer            User needs, usability and interaction flow         How will users complete their tasks easily?

UI Designer            Visual interface and presentation                  How should the interface look?


An important distinction: Product Design is a broader discipline. UX and UI are important parts of Product Design. In some companies, one Product
Designer handles UX and UI together; in others, these responsibilities are split across specialists.


2. Example: Building a food delivery app
Imagine a company wants to build a new food delivery app.
  behance.net



A. Product Manager — Decides what and why

Problem: Customers are abandoning their food orders before completing payment.

The Product Manager investigates the business problem, customer feedback and product data.

Responsibilities:

    Understand business goals and customer problems.
    Define product vision and strategy.
    Prioritise features.
    Prepare product roadmap and requirements.
    Coordinate business, design and engineering teams.
    Define success metrics.

Example decision:

"We need to simplify the checkout process to reduce order abandonment and improve completed orders."

Possible success metrics:

    Checkout completion rate
    Order conversion rate
    Revenue per order
    Customer retention


B. Product Designer — Owns the end-to-end experience

The Product Designer translates the problem into a complete product experience.

Responsibilities:

    Understand user and business needs.
    Conduct or collaborate on user research.
    Define the problem and design strategy.
    Create user journeys and information architecture.
    Design user flows, wireframes and prototypes.
    Create visual UI and design systems.
    Validate designs through usability testing.
    Collaborate with developers and Product Managers.

Example decision:
"Let's create a simpler checkout experience with fewer steps, clear pricing and multiple payment options."

The Product Designer connects user needs, business goals and technical feasibility.


C. UX Designer — Focuses on usability and user behaviour

The UX Designer focuses on how easily users can complete their tasks.

Responsibilities:

    User research and interviews.
    Personas and user journeys.
    Information architecture.
    User flows and task flows.
    Wireframes and interaction design.
    Usability testing.
    Accessibility and experience improvements.

Example decision:

"Users are confused by the delivery address and payment steps. Let's reduce unnecessary steps and make the order summary easier to understand."


D. UI Designer — Focuses on visual design

The UI Designer makes the interface visually clear, consistent and attractive.

Responsibilities:

    Layout and visual hierarchy.
    Typography and colour systems.
    Buttons, icons and components.
    Spacing and visual consistency.
    Responsive interface design.
    Design system and UI specifications.

Example decision:

"Let's make the total price more prominent, improve button contrast and use a consistent visual style across checkout screens."


3. How all four roles work together
Here is a simplified product development workflow.


  1 Product Manager
      Identifies the business problem and defines product priorities.
      Output: Product vision, roadmap, requirements, priorities

  2 Product Designer
      Understands the problem and shapes the overall product experience.
      Output: Design strategy, experience concepts, prototypes

  3 UX Designer
      Defines the structure, user journey and interaction flow.
      Output: User research, journeys, IA, wireframes, usability findings

  4 UI Designer
      Creates the visual interface and reusable components.
      Output: High-fidelity screens, visual design, UI specifications

  5 Engineering Team
      Develops and implements the product, working with the team throughout.
      Output: Functional product

  6 Product Team
      Measures results, learns from users and improves the product.


Note: This is a conceptual workflow, not a strict handoff process. In modern product teams, these roles collaborate continuously and overlap.

8. Interview preparation: A strong answer
If an interviewer asks, "What is the difference between a Product Manager and a Product Designer?", you can answer:


   A Product Manager primarily focuses on product strategy, business goals, priorities and deciding what problems the product should solve.

   A Product Designer focuses on translating those problems into meaningful user experiences by understanding user needs, defining workflows,
   designing interactions and creating user-friendly interfaces.

   UX Design focuses more on usability and the overall user journey, while UI Design focuses on the visual presentation and interface.

   In modern product teams, these roles collaborate closely. As a Product Designer, I believe my responsibility is to balance user needs, business
   objectives and technical feasibility to create successful digital products.
